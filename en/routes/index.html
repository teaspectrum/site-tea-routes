Asia

The Southern Silk Road Tea Route

The Southern Silk Road is a multi-era network from the Chengdu plain through Sichuan and Yunnan into Myanmar and northeast India, not a single ancient highway and not a route that carried tea from end to end. This article follows a 32-station reading line from Chengdu through Ya'an, Xichang, Dali, Baoshan, Tengchong, Bhamo, Myitkyina, the Pangsau Pass and the Assam valley to Guwahati, separating the Han-era Shu-Shendu corridor, later tea-horse trade, Qing and Republican Pu'er exports, the nineteenth-century Assam plantation industry and the twentieth-century Stilwell Road. It rejects the claims of a fourth-century-BCE tea road, direct Yunnan-to-Assam tea-seed transmission and an Ahom origin for Assamese tea drinking.
